Seven screens, drawn at phone width, in the app's own tokens. The law is the Blade — one sharp corner, three round, on every surface. Sky is the one action colour and always carries white text. Grape means a machine wrote it. Nothing here is a mock of a mock: every value below exists in web/src/index.css.

The land
The button is the only thing with --drop weight on this screen, so the eye has exactly one destination. "No account" is placed where a pricing link used to be.
Design decisions

Why this replaces seven scroll sections

  • The headline asks a question the visitor is already asking. "What are you eating today?" is the internal monologue; the page just says it out loud.
  • The illustration carries the mechanism — a pot with a question mark rising from it says "we answer this" faster than any subhead.
  • One CTA, no secondary. "Sign in" is a quiet text button in the corner because returning users know to look there.
  • The proof line is a count, not a claim. A number is checkable; "loved by thousands" is not.

Mood

Why drawn faces, not emoji
  • Emoji render differently on every platform and carry their vendor's art style into a page with a strong one of its own.
  • They take the brand's line weight. These use currentColor and inherit the tile's state, so a picked face goes sky-blue with everything else.
  • Each face has a food cue — the chef's hat, the lightning bolt, the heart — so the tile reads even with the label removed.
Motion

On tap: kj-pop — scale(.9) → 1.04 → 1 over --t-base with the overshoot ease. The tile arrives like a plate set down. Nothing on this screen loops.

The verdict
The single tallest screen in the flow, and the only one that scrolls. Everything above the fold is the answer; the signup band sits deliberately below it.
The anatomy
ElementWhy it is there
"Chosen for you"
grape chip		 Grape is reserved system-wide for AI provenance — never a status, never a button. It tells the truth about who wrote the sentence below.
"5 of 7"
success chip		 The deterministic match score, stated as a fraction. Checkable, and it comes from matchMeal(), not the model.
The name
24px Baloo 2 800		 The largest text on the screen. No hedging verbs — the product's job is to decide, so it decides.
The why
model-written, ≤200 chars		 Must reference at least one real thing they told us. This is what separates a recommendation from a random pick.
Have / need
green / amber split		 Two boxes side by side, never one list with icons — the shopping decision is "how much is missing", answerable at a glance.
Alternates
34px minis, no art		 Subordinate by construction: small, no hero plate, no why. Tapping re-ranks locally and spends no model call.
Not feeling it?
ghost, under primary		 Rejects this dish and promotes the next from the shortlist the response already carried. Costs no model call, so the commonest "no" is free.
Change an answer
quiet, in the header		 Reopens one step rather than the whole flow. Also free — the candidate pool is re-ranked locally against the new answer.
Keep band
sky-soft, below CTA		 The first ask in the entire flow, and it arrives after the value. "No thanks" is present and unpunished.
What is deliberately absent No steps, no quantities, no star rating, no "87% match". The verdict proves we can decide; the recipe is what an account buys. And a percentage implies a precision the matcher does not have — "5 of 7" is the honest version of the same fact.
Rate-limit refusal, as a screen

On 429 the flow does not show an error. It shows the offer, with the real wait from Retry-After:

That's a few decisions in one go Another one in 12 minutes — or make an account and we'll stop counting.

The limit becomes the pitch. A refusal that routes somewhere useful is not a dead end, and it costs nothing to build — the header is already exposed through CORS in app.ts.

After a rejection
The acknowledgement names the dish that was refused, so it reads as listening rather than reshuffling. The grape chip is gone — a promoted meal shows a templated line, never the AI "why" written about a different dish.
The four ways out
ControlDoesCost
Not feeling it? Excludes this dish, promotes the next candidate. Never offers it again this session. free
Tap an alternate Swaps that one into the hero slot; the current winner drops into the list. free
Change an answer Reopens one step — usually mood or time. Everything else is kept. free
Start again Back to step 1, keeping only the rejections. 1 token
Three of the four are free The decide response carries the whole ranked shortlist, not just the winner. Re-ranking six objects already in the browser is arithmetic — so the things a person is most likely to do cost nothing, and only a full restart spends a token.
When the shortlist runs out
That's everything that fits Nothing left for 40 minutes with what you have. Loosen the time, or add one more thing from your kitchen.

Routes back to a step, not to a dead end — widening the filter is exactly what produces new candidates, so the suggestion is actionable.

Story card 1 of 3
The image earns most of the screen. The bolded line is a deterministic fact about their kitchen; the line under it is the model's one-sentence pitch.
What each card carries
SlotSourceRule
ImageGenerated hero Falls back to the drawn glyph on a tinted plate when absent — the carousel works with no imagery at all.
Namemeal.nameNever truncated.
The hookmatchMeal() Bolded, deterministic. "You have 5 of 7" · "Two shops away" · "Fastest of the three". Arithmetic, never a guess.
The pitchModel, ≤120 charsOne line on why you'd want it. No exclamation marks.
The costDerived Only when one genuinely exists — missing items, long cook time. Omitted entirely when the dish is cookable now.
Pros and cons, but not symmetrically A balanced pros/cons list on three dishes hands the decision straight back — the thing this flow exists to prevent — and "cons" on food reads as talking someone out of dinner. So: an upside always, a cost only where real, and nothing invented for symmetry. A dish with no cost line is making the strongest argument on the screen by omission. Same rule as UserFacingNotesSchema: omit what has nothing to say.
Three, hard maximum

A carousel of equals is a menu, and a menu is the problem the person arrived with. Card one keeps the largest treatment and the only fully model-written why; cards two and three are arguments, not peers.

The voice

The person is undecided, a little tired, probably hungry. The register is a friend who cooks — warm, quick, faintly funny, never in the way. Two failure modes to avoid: the perky app mascot, and the try-hard brand voice.

Do
  • Short beats clever. "Not feeling it?" over "Let's find you something more your vibe!" Length is what makes copy annoying, not wit.
  • Be playful about the situation, never the food. Teasing an empty kitchen is warm; teasing someone's dinner is not.
  • The verdict declares. The product's value is deciding, so it must sound decided.
  • One joke per screen, maximum. Usually zero — playfulness is seasoning, and a whole flow of it is exhausting to walk twice.
Don't
  • No exclamation marks in model-written text — it is what separates warm from perky, and it is already in the prompt.
  • No emoji in product copy. 187 drawn icons with a consistent line weight; emoji break it.
  • Never joke in an error. Someone whose upload failed wants the fact and the fix.
  • No pet names. No "chef", no "foodie", no "fam".

The Blade, demonstrated

One sharp corner, three round, clockwise from top-left: round · sharp · round · sharp. It reads as a knife edge, a chopping-board corner, a torn recipe card. Four round corners, or two sharp, is not in this system.

The sharp corner stays small and constant so the cut reads at every scale. Pills and circles are the only exceptions — the chips and the tick badge.

Depth is a solid edge, not a blur

The object presses down onto its own edge. Blur is reserved for the overlay layer only — which is why nothing in this flow has a soft shadow.

09

Build notes

Reachability
  • Every tile is a real <button> with aria-pressed, not a styled div.
  • The focus ring is the system's: a 2px paper gap then 4px sky, so it survives on any background.
  • Each step is its own route, so Back is the browser's Back and needs no interception.
  • Tap targets are --h-md (46px) or larger throughout. No 32px targets on a phone.
Never colour alone
  • A picked tile carries a tick badge as well as the sky fill.
  • Have/need are labelled in words, with different icons, not just green and amber.
  • The grape chip says "Chosen for you" — the colour reinforces, it does not inform.
  • The progress rail is paired with a 2 / 4 readout.
Motion
  • Only two things loop: the thinking band and the steam. Both stop on arrival.
  • prefers-reduced-motion collapses every duration to 0.01ms — already in index.css.
  • Step transitions are kj-slide-left forward, kj-slide-right back, so direction is legible.
  • No parallax, no scroll-jacking. The flow is taps, not scrolling.
Illustrations in this file are inline SVG placeholders They are correct in structure, colour and line weight, and shippable as-is. Where a richer illustration is wanted — the hero pot, the four mood faces, the hero dish — the illustrations/ folder carries one self-contained, paste-ready prompt file per slot, keyed to the ILLUSTRATION SLOT comments in this file's source.
