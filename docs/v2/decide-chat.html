Decide, as a conversation

A proposal to re-house the five-step decide wizard inside a chat transcript — without turning a four-tap flow into a typing exercise. Same questions, same endpoint, same verdict. What changes is that the flow becomes something you can see, go back into, and talk to.

Light mode only Tokens unchanged from design.html Functional parity held 4 new retention mechanics Ships behind a flag
01

What I actually think

"Make it more AI-ish" is a request I want to push back on for one sentence before I agree with it. A chat UI that is just the wizard with speech bubbles drawn around it is strictly worse than what we have: more scrolling, less scannable, slower to the verdict, and it invites people to type when tapping was already faster. Plenty of products shipped that and quietly regressed conversion.

But there are three things the wizard genuinely cannot do, and all three are chat's native strengths. Those are worth the rebuild.

Reason one

The flow becomes a record

Right now "Change an answer" means leaving the verdict and re-entering a step you can't see. In a transcript every answer you gave is still on screen, above you. Tapping one re-opens it in place, and the correction is appended rather than hidden — so you never lose your position or wonder what the system thinks you said.

This is the one that matters most. It is the current flow's sharpest edge.

Reason two

Dead air becomes personality

Between steps the wizard shows nothing. A transcript has a speaker who can react to what you just said — "Garri and panla. That already opens a few doors." It costs no model call (these are deterministic, written responses keyed to the answer), and it is the single cheapest way to make the product feel like it is paying attention.

Voice rules from design.html §07 still bind: short beats clever, never a joke about the food, no exclamation marks.

Reason three

A shortcut lane opens

A chat container gives us somewhere honest to put free text. A cook who types "rice, one tomato, I'm exhausted, 20 minutes" has answered four questions in one line. We parse it, fill the tiles, and show them filled so they can correct us. Taps stay the default; typing is the power move.

Also unlocks voice, which is the right input for someone standing in a kitchen.

The design rule everything below follows

Taps stay primary. Typing is the shortcut. Chat is the container — not the input method.

Concretely: the bottom of the screen is a tap surface that can also take text, never a bare text field. The thread above is the transcript. If we ever ship a frame where the only way to answer is to type a sentence, we have broken the rule and slowed the product down.

What I am deliberately not changing

  • The questions and their order — kitchen, mood, weight, time. Same four, same DECIDE_STAGES.
  • The single model call. One POST /decide, same payload shape. Chat does not mean chatty API.
  • Local re-ranking. "Not feeling it" still promotes from pool and costs nothing.
  • The draft in sessionStorage, v1 shape untouched. A transcript is rendered from the draft, not stored alongside it.
  • One invite, one dismissal. inviteStore rules hold exactly. A chat format must not become licence to ask twice.
  • The rate limit copy — the refusal is still the offer.
  • Order mode swaps the time question for the place question, as today.
  • The blade. Bubbles get blade radii, never pills. This is the detail that keeps it looking like us and not like every other chatbot.

04

The three states we must not make cute

A conversational voice is at its most dangerous when something has gone wrong. The rule from design.html §07 — never a joke inside an error — gets stricter here, because a bubble reads as a person talking. These three keep their current copy word for word.

Rate limited
That's a lot of deciding

Another one in 3 minutes, or make an account and we'll stop counting.

The refusal is the offer. No apology bubble, no typing dots pretending to work. A countdown that ticks in the header, and a real way past it.

Exhausted
That's everything that fits

Nothing left that matches what you asked for. Loosen the time, or tell us one more thing you have at home.

In a transcript this is better than today: "change an answer" points at bubbles that are still on screen, so the fix is one tap away instead of a step away.

Nothing fits
Nothing quite fits

Nothing matched that combination of time and taste. Try loosening one of them.

Names the two answers at fault and makes both tappable. The pot does not say sorry — it says which lever to pull.

Hard rule for engineering: no state may show a typing indicator unless a request is genuinely in flight. Fake thinking to seem smart is the one thing in this redesign that would actively damage trust.
05

Four new mechanics the chat format pays for

Each of these is impossible or awkward in the wizard and natural in a transcript. I have ordered them by expected return against build cost. Only the first two are in scope for v1.

Ship in v1Cheap

1 · Push back on the verdict

Three standing quick replies under every verdict — Not feeling it, Make it faster, Less pepper — plus free text. Each one is a filter over the pool we already hold, so each costs zero model calls.

Why it retains: the current flow's only rejection path is "try again", which feels like a dice roll. Pushing back feels like being heard, and a cook who pushes back twice and then cooks has had a far better session than one who accepted the first thing.

Measure: share of sessions with ≥1 push-back, and whether those sessions convert to Cook this at a higher rate than single-shot sessions.
Ship in v1Cheap

2 · The thread resumes

A returning cook does not land on a cold hero. They land on their transcript with a new pot message at the bottom: "Last time you had panla and went soupy. Same kitchen tonight?" One tap re-uses the saved kitchen and jumps to mood.

Why it retains: this is the habit loop the wizard structurally cannot have. A wizard always starts at step one. A conversation picks up. We already store everything needed — use-my-kitchen and DecideHistoryEntry hold the kitchen and the answers.

Measure: D7 return rate, and taps-to-verdict for returning versus new cooks.
v2Needs a parser

3 · One-line answers

Frame 04. A cook types or speaks one sentence and we fill every tile we can read from it, then show the fill so it can be corrected. Voice is the real prize — the decide moment happens with hands occupied.

Why it is v2 and not v1: it needs a parse step with a real failure mode, and a bad parse is worse than no parse. It wants its own flag and its own metric before it goes near the default path.

Risk: if parse accuracy sits below roughly 85% on the chip set, the correction tax exceeds the taps saved and we should not ship it.
v2Account only

4 · The morning message

The invite already promises "a fresh idea every morning, before you ask". A transcript gives that promise somewhere to land: a pot message waiting in the thread, with the kitchen it assumed and a one-tap yes.

Why it retains: it turns the product from a tool you remember to open into a thread that has something in it. It is also the honest reason to make an account, which makes the invite in frame 07 easier to say yes to.

Guardrail: one message a day, no badge, no push until someone has cooked at least twice. A thread that nags is a thread people mute.
06

What could go wrong, and the mitigation

I would not ship this unflagged. The wizard is a known quantity with known numbers; a transcript is a bet. Here is what I am actually worried about.

RiskWhy it is realMitigation
Slower to verdict Chat UIs invite reading. If median time-to-verdict rises, we have made the product worse at its one job no matter how good it feels. Auto-advance on tap removes three Continue presses, so taps actually go down. Gate the rollout on time-to-verdict, not on sentiment.
People type instead of tapping A visible composer is an invitation. Typing is slower than tapping and parsing can fail. Composer is visually secondary — smaller, grey, under the tiles. Placeholder reads "Or just tell me", with the "or" doing real work.
The voice gets twee Reaction lines are where a brand voice goes to die. Thirty written lines is thirty chances to be annoying on the second read. Reactions are a reviewable copy file like decide.content.ts, under the same §07 rules. Anything that would not survive being read five times gets cut.
Scroll jank on small phones A growing thread plus a dock that changes height is the classic mobile chat bug — content jumps as the keyboard opens. Dock height is fixed per stage and never animates. Thread scrolls to the newest pot message, not to the bottom. Dock dims rather than unmounting.
It reads as a chatbot Generic bubbles would throw away the visual identity that makes this product memorable. The blade holds on every bubble. No pill radii, no grey-on-grey, hard drop shadows throughout. Compare any frame above to a stock chat UI.
Accessibility regression A live-updating transcript is a screen-reader hazard — chatty regions announce constantly. Thread is aria-live="polite" with system-rail entries excluded. Each stage keeps a real heading. Full keyboard path through the dock before launch.
07

How I would sequence it

Four stages. Each one is shippable and each one is measurable on its own, so we can stop after any of them without having built something half-finished.

Stage one

The shell

Transcript renderer over the existing draft, three bubble kinds, the dock, the header meter. Reuses every step component as dock content — the tiles do not get rewritten.

No new copy, no new endpoint. Behind decide_chat.

Stage two

Edit and react

Tappable cook bubbles with later answers preserved, the strike-through record, and the reaction copy file. This is the stage that justifies the rebuild.

A/B against the wizard here. Gate: time-to-verdict flat or better.

Stage three

Push back

Verdict quick replies as local filters over pool, the alternates strip, and the thread that resumes for signed-in cooks.

Still zero extra model calls. Gate: D7 return rate.

Stage four

Free text and voice

The parser, the editable-chip confirmation, the disagreement rail, and the mic. Its own flag, its own accuracy bar.

Only if stage three cleared its gate. Easiest thing to cut.

What I need from engineering to size this

  • Can the existing step components (step-kitchen, step-mood, step-weight, step-time) render inside a fixed-height dock without a rewrite? If yes, stage one is small.
  • Does editing an earlier answer without re-running later stages fit the current use-decide reducer, or does the draft need a per-field dirty flag?
  • Are Make it faster and Less pepper expressible as predicates over DecideMeal in decide-rerank — cook_time_minutes and tags — or do they need server support?
  • Is there an existing aria-live pattern in the app I should match, or is this the first live region?
My recommendation: build stages one and two, A/B them honestly, and hold three and four until the first two have earned it. The transcript is worth building for the edit affordance alone — everything conversational on top of that is upside, not the point.
