The Open App

The product stops being a walled garden. Deciding is the front door for everyone, signed in or not; an account changes what the app remembers, not whether you may use it. Four changes, one shape.

Signup moves into the flow Signed in skips the kitchen step Decide becomes the default tab AI audit gets real numbers
00

One idea behind all four

These read as four separate asks. They are one: the account stops being a gate and becomes an upgrade. Everything below follows from that, which is why they should ship together rather than as four patches.

Today

An account is a toll gate

  • /kitchen and everything past it demand a session. A visitor who wants to look gets a login screen.
  • Signup is offered once, after the verdict. Somebody who bounces at step two is never asked.
  • A signed-in cook is asked what is in their kitchen, which the app already knows from their stock.
  • The kitchen dashboard is the landing surface, so the one thing the product does best is two taps from the front door.
After

An account is an upgrade

  • The flow is the front door for everybody. Signed in, it is the default tab.
  • Signup is offered inside the flow, at the moment there is something to lose, and it never interrupts.
  • A signed-in cook's kitchen is pre-filled from their stock. The step becomes a confirmation, not data entry.
  • The dashboard stays, one tap away, for the people who want it.
The measurable claim Two new conversion points (the in-flow invite, and the nav for a returning guest) and one removed step for signed-in cooks. Every one is already instrumented by decide_logs.

The invite, inside the flow

A popup during a flow is usually a mistake: it interrupts somebody who is mid-task to ask for something they have no reason to give yet. This one works only if it obeys three rules — it arrives after value has landed, it never blocks, and it never asks twice.

01
The invite
A bottom sheet, not a centred modal: it sits where the thumb is, and it visibly does not replace the screen underneath. The step behind stays legible.

The three rules

  1. Never before value. It cannot appear on the hero or on step one. The earliest is after the mood answer, when they have invested something.
  2. Never blocking. Dismiss, tap the scrim, or press Escape and the flow continues exactly where it was. No answer is lost.
  3. Never twice. One dismissal ends it for the session. A second ask is how a helpful offer becomes a nuisance.

When it fires

TriggerWhy there
After step 3
the default
They have answered three questions. Sunk cost is real, the verdict is one tap away, and losing it now would sting.
On a second decision Somebody who decides twice in one session is the likeliest to want it remembered.
Never on the verdict The verdict already has its own keep-band. Two asks on one screen is nagging.
The honest risk An interstitial mid-flow is exactly the pattern that makes people leave. It is worth doing ONLY because it is dismissible, single-shot, and arrives after investment. Ship it behind a flag and compare step-3→verdict completion with and without it. If completion drops more than signups rise, it is a bad trade and it comes out.
What it stores

One key in sessionStorage, beside the draft: kj.invite_dismissed. Session-scoped for the same reason the draft is — a shared laptop should not carry one person's choice into the next person's visit.

Signing up without leaving

The sheet holds the real register form, not a link to one. Navigating away mid-flow to sign up and then finding your way back is the friction this is supposed to remove.

Signed in: three steps, not four
The kitchen becomes a confirmation strip at the bottom, with a way to override. Stated rather than silent, so nobody wonders what it used.

Where their kitchen comes from

Their stock, not their onboarding answers. Onboarding is a snapshot from the day they joined; stock is what they actually have now, and it is what every other screen already trusts. 

// GET /api/v1/stock  →  the names, deduped
const mine = stock.items
  .filter((i) => i.quantity > 0)
  .map((i) => i.name);
  • Pre-filled, never forced. "Change" reopens the normal picker with their stock pre-selected.
  • Stated on screen. A count and three examples, so it is obvious what the answer was built from.
  • Empty stock falls back. A signed-in cook with nothing recorded sees the ordinary step. Four steps, same as a guest.

The rate limit changes too DECIDE_ANON is 8/hour keyed by IP, which is right for a stranger and wrong for a paying-attention cook behind a shared NAT. A signed-in request should key by user id and use a looser policy. Same endpoint, different bucket — the middleware's byIdentity resolver already does exactly this.

Where sign-in lands

  • Sign in → /, the flow, not the kitchen. It is the default tab, so landing anywhere else contradicts that.
  • Onboarding still runs for a fresh account with no draft. A guest who converts skips it, because their draft already answered it.

The one thing to get right RouteGuard currently sends an unauthenticated visitor to /login. After this change that is correct for /kitchen and wrong for a recipe page. The guard needs a third mode — public, better signed in — or every recipe URL keeps 302-ing Google to a login form.

Cost, per prompt

Tokens × the model's own rate. lib/analytics/ai-cost.ts already exists, so this is a read rather than new maths.

  • Spend by prompt id, so the expensive one is obvious
  • Spend by model, so a tier change is visible
  • Cost per decision — the only cost figure that means anything

Health, per prompt

  • Parse rate. A prompt that stops parsing is a prompt that broke.
  • p50 and p95 duration. The mean hides the timeouts; p95 is what people feel.
  • Self-graded metrics. outputConfidence and ambiguity are already stored and never surfaced.

Rising ambiguity on one prompt is the earliest signal a prompt needs work.

Provider and model

  • Split by provider, so a mock-vs-live mix is visible at a glance
  • Split by model, so a silent fallback to a cheaper tier is caught
  • Fallback count: how often a call failed and the deterministic path answered
Mock in production If provider: mock ever appears on a production chart, something is badly wrong and nothing currently says so.
All of it is already in the database ai_logs stores prompt id, provider, model, token counts, duration, parse outcome and the self-graded metrics on every call. This section is aggregation and presentation — no new writes, no new collection, no schema change.

The guard's third mode

Today it is binary: signed in, or go to login. A recipe page is neither. 

// route-guard.tsx
type Access =
  | 'private'   // kitchen, stock, market
  | 'public'    // the flow, marketing
  | 'open';    // a recipe: renders either way,
               // and the ACTIONS ask for a signup
  • private — unchanged behaviour
  • open — never redirects; the screen decides what a guest may do
Audit every private route deliberately Opening routes is a one-way door: a screen that leaks another person's data cannot be un-leaked. Each route moves to open only after somebody has decided what a stranger may see on it.
06

Build order

Sequenced so the cheap, reversible things ship first and the one-way door is opened last, with the most review.

#StageContainsDone when
1AI audit The stats endpoint and the new screen. Read-only, admin-only. The page answers what it costs and which prompt is failing. Zero risk to anything public.
2Nav reorder Decide first, Kitchen second, sign-in lands on the flow. A signed-in cook lands on Decide. Nothing else moved.
3Skip the kitchen step Pre-fill from stock, the confirmation strip, the 3-step rail, DECIDE_USER. A signed-in cook is never asked what they already told us.
4The invite, behind a flag The sheet, the dismissal key, in-sheet register, carry-over. Flag on: it appears once, dismissible, and signup keeps the draft. Flag off: nothing changes.
5Measure it Compare step-3→verdict completion, flag on vs off, in decide_logs. A decision to keep it or drop it, made on numbers rather than taste.
6Open the recipe pages The guard's third mode, public GET /meals/:id, a per-route audit. A recipe URL renders for a stranger, and every remaining private route was checked on purpose.
Why the audit page goes first It is the only item with no public blast radius, it is entirely additive, and it is the thing that tells you what stages 2–6 did to your AI spend. Shipping it first means the rest is measurable from day one.
07

What I would push back on

Three of these four are straightforwardly good. One is a real trade, and one has a failure mode worth naming before it is built rather than after.

The trade

A mid-flow popup costs completions

Interrupting a four-step flow to ask for an email is the pattern people describe when they say a site is annoying. It will convert some people, and it will lose others at step three who would have reached a verdict.

I am not arguing against it — the sunk-cost timing is genuinely the right moment, and it is dismissible. But it must ship behind a flag with the completion rate watched, because "it felt like it helped" is not something you can tell without the number.

The number that decides it step-3 → verdict completion, flag on versus flag off. If it drops more than signups rise, the popup is costing more than it earns.
The one-way door

Opening routes cannot be undone

Once a URL has served a stranger, assume it is indexed and archived. The mitigation is not caution in general but a route-by-route decision: for each one, what may a stranger see, and what must still ask for a signup.

  • A recipe page is safe: the catalogue is the product's public face.
  • /meals/:id must not leak favourited or cooked state for the owner.
  • /kitchen, /stock, /market, /week stay private. There is nothing in them that is not somebody's own data.
A smaller thing worth deciding now With Decide as the default tab, what does a returning cook see when they have already decided today? Re-asking four questions is worse than useless. The flow should recognise a decision made in the last few hours and open on it, with "decide again" as the secondary action. That is a small change now and an awkward retrofit later.
And the product tour product-tour.tsx starts at ROUTES.KITCHEN and walks through the kitchen dashboard. If Decide becomes the default tab and the tour still opens on Kitchen, a new account is told the wrong thing is the point. The tour needs rewriting in stage 2, not later.
